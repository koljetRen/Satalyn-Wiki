# World Creation Notes

## The Cosmic Order

- A **Supreme Creator** (Euiklatz, the God of the Eternities) rules over the multiverse.
- Beneath him are lesser supreme beings in a caste system: those who rule galaxies, those who rule worlds, and a lowest caste with little power or standing.
- A being who becomes prestigious enough may ask the Supreme Creator for a world of their own. His one condition: its people must worship him as the creator.
- All creation follows the natural laws in the Supreme Creator's **Book of Creation**, which roughly match how Earth formed.

## The Creation of This World

- Lebarka, the God of Life, asked for a world, and the Supreme Creator granted him an empty region of space. The world is currently called Satalyn, a placeholder name to be replaced.
- This deity calls himself the **Father of All**. The Supreme Creator also gave him a partner, the **Mother of All, Modingua, Goddess of Motherhood, love, fertility and reproduction, who decides which humanoid races are placed on the world**. She loves every race equally. Tensions between races come from life in the world, not from divine favouritism.
- Only the Supreme Creator builds anything. The Father of All passes requests up the chain on behalf of himself and the other gods.

### The Seven Phases of Creation

The Book of Creation sets out seven phases that every world goes through, a blend of Earth's natural history and the seven days of Genesis.

**Timing:** each phase takes as long as the Supreme Creator decides. It isn't millions of years, and it isn't one literal day. No mortals existed to count the time, so in legend the phases are simply ages.

**No phase creates the stars.** They already exist: each star is the creation of another being of the Father of All's caste who asked the Supreme Creator for a world of their own.

**Phases 1 to 4: the Supreme Creator builds the world alone.** The Father of All's only input is where things are placed.

1. **Light:** the whole solar system forms. A young sun is lit, and the world, three bare moons and the other planets pull together from the same dust and fire.
2. **Sky and waters:** the Supreme Creator wraps the bare rock world in a great bubble of water. The bubble bursts, leaving behind the sky and atmosphere, and its water pours down until one ocean covers the whole surface. The rock is still settling, so the world shakes with earthquakes. The tectonic plates begin to form, and the first landmasses start to take shape.
3. **Land:** the land breaks through the ocean and the continents take shape. The constant earthquakes die down as the tectonic plates settle into large plates. The world is still active, though: the big plates now drift slowly, colliding and raising mountains.
4. **Climate and seasons:** the Creator tilts the world and sets its spin, which gives it seasons, winds, ocean currents and weather patterns, and so its climates.
5. **Life:** for the first time, the Supreme Creator asks the Father of All what changes he wants, and they fine-tune the land together. Following his rules for what lives in each climate, the Creator makes simple plant life and animals. Then he makes the humanoid races the Mother of All asks for (elves, humans, or whatever she chooses) and places them around the world.

**Phase 6: the requests.** The Father of All gathers the Nine's requests and brings them to the Supreme Creator, who makes them real. This phase covers every request other than life itself. The Father of All asks the Nine for their requests and adds a few of his own (to be filled in from Jaye's notes on the gods). Magic is given to its first cult in this phase. Stomell's three daughters are placed into the bare moons, and Felderg colours and shapes the simple plant life into forests, deserts, tundra and snowy peaks. The world is still mild and gentle, a golden age.

**Phase 7: the Creator steps away.** His influence on the world becomes minimal to none. By the rules of the Book, every world's seventh phase begins with a great freeze, so the ice age arrives right after life does. The ice age lasts roughly 1,000 years, far shorter than Earth's, because it is a designed part of creation. There are no ice caps at the end of phase six. Once the freeze begins, ice sheets spread over the north and south within 100 to 150 years: fast, but slow enough that people can flee rather than die instantly. After about 1,000 years the ice age ends, and the ice takes another 150 to 200 years to retreat to its present-day extent.

- The Nine come from the lowest caste and didn't know this rule, so they planned their gifts for a mild world that was about to freeze. This is where many of their mistakes come from.
- The Father of All almost certainly knew, since he had asked for a world and would have seen the rules in action.
- Story hook: the ice age people may remember a warm golden age before the ice.
- Why the Father of All didn't warn the Nine: partly he assumed they already knew, since the freeze is common knowledge at his rank. It was also a quiet test of whether they could really rise to a higher caste.

## The Nine

- The Father and Mother of All lifted nine deities from the lowest caste, giving them a chance they would never otherwise have had.
- Each could pick **one domain**: the single thing they believed would give them the most power. The full list is below. Names may change, and each god is known by different names in different regions.
- Some chose badly. Their flawed choices can explain flaws in the world and rivalry among the gods.
- That makes twelve deities in the pantheon: the Supreme Creator, the Father, the Mother and the Nine.

### The Nine (draft names)

1. **Dauviti, God of the Dead:** gave every creature the ability to die. Mortals plead for mercy in the afterlife, so each culture's beliefs about death have a chance of coming true. He has no real flaw: death is necessary. Mortals see creating death as his flaw, but he came from the top of the lowest caste, understood the rules, and knew exactly what he was doing.
2. **Stomell, God of the Skies:** his three daughters became the world's three moons. He controls the weather but kindly keeps it to predictable patterns, usually. Idea: he gave mortals knowledge of the seasons and stars for planting and navigation. His flaw: people trust the patterns so completely that they're caught out when the weather breaks.
3. **Ozefio, Goddess of the Seas:** gave mortals shipbuilding and fishing. Her flaw: she didn't realise the sea is ruled by Stomell's weather, so early sailors were lost to storms. Sailors pray to her for safe passage. Her pets live in the seas and inspire coastal tales of sea monsters. Lost ships are said to have lost her favour.
4. **Felderg, God of the Earth:** now leaning towards a God of the Harvest. An artist who coloured the world's plant life, he gave mortals the knowledge of plants and farming. His flaw: he didn't think about poisonous plants, so people sometimes harmed themselves by accident.
5. **Gehflaun, Goddess of Intelligence:** gave mortals reading, writing and study. Central to worship among the Dyken people. Her flaw: she innocently believed knowledge would simply make everyone happy, and never imagined it could divide people. That left the opening for Krarkur, who twisted intelligence into castes and discrimination. This is felt across the world, but most strongly among the Dyken, whose society relies on intelligence.
6. **Dreplagen, God of Strength:** believes strength beats intelligence. Gave mortals the ability to build strength, speed and mobility, working with Felderg on food and nourishment. His flaws: strength let people take what they wanted, leading to the first conflicts and wars, and he didn't realise bodies pushed hard would wear down with injury and exhaustion.
7. **Krarkur, God of Deceit:** corrupted the gifts of intelligence and strength, tempting people into social tiers and spreading hate and competition. His gift: persuasion, negotiation and cunning, the skills behind trade, diplomacy and leadership, which also make lies possible. Like Dauviti, he came from the top of the lowest caste and knew exactly what he was doing.
8. **Klist, Goddess of Music:** gave the world sound, including creatures' voices, music in the wind and thunder, and languages. Inspires people to make instruments. A natural fit for dance-and-song worship. Idea: language lets everyone cooperate from the start. Her flaw: words can also wound and spread lies, which hands Krarkur his best tool.
9. **Magurkai, God of Magic:** visited 100 people from a village and gave them magic. They draw on the iron in their blood to cast, so he changed their bodies to store and produce more iron. Only they and their descendants can wield it. The purer the bloodline, the more iron the blood can hold, and so the greater the magic. As the line thins, magic weakens until descendants can't cast at all, though rarely a dormant trait resurfaces. His flaws: overcasting drains iron and can cause fainting or death, and the bloodline splits the world into the magic-born and everyone else.

**Theme of the gifts:** each phase six gift pushes mortals far ahead, so they skip the hunter-gatherer stage and start farming and building cities right away. Each gift also has a hidden flaw its god didn't foresee. The Nine didn't coordinate, so some gifts clash, like Ozefio's ships and Stomell's storms.

**Ice age twist:** the first cities rise, then phase seven's freeze hits. Fields freeze over and early cities are abandoned, so the ice age people are a fallen civilisation, not cavemen.

**Love belongs to Modingua, the Mother of All, not one of the Nine**.

Other lesser deities exist too, worshipped by small cults or indigenous peoples, but only these twelve are known worldwide. The pantheon still needs a name.

## The Sacred Creatures

- People believe that for each god there is one majestic creature living on the world. In truth, the creatures don't live on the world: they are the gods' true forms in the realm of the multiverse the gods come from.
- Each has the body of a whale, the antlers of an elk, and fins that work like an owl's wings. They can swim the ocean depths and also fly.
- Each creature's antlers are unique and reflect the personality of the god it represents.
- No one has ever confirmed seeing one on the world, but no one can disprove them either. They remain a rumour and a theory.
- Possible gods' forms: each race tends to picture the gods in its own image, while the creatures and shared symbols are common to every culture.
- Mortals know none of this. To them the creatures are only rumoured sacred beasts, perhaps a faint memory of the gods' true shape.
- **How mortals know of them:** like the Loch Ness Monster. People claim glimpses, such as a vast shape breaching far out at sea or antlers against the clouds, but there is never proof. These sightings happen on the rare occasions a god is called back to the world, so a sighting is really a god passing through.

## How the Gods Relate to Mortals

- The gods are mostly **distant**. They designed the world but never built it with their own hands.
- **Twist:** each god keeps a small amount of power, and only inside their own domain. The god of love can't end a drought.
- The Supreme Creator, Father and Mother are honoured everywhere, in a formal, distant way.
- The Nine are worshipped by region, as in the Greek pantheon. A water-dwelling people, such as possible merfolk, would favour the water god. The water god may have chosen where the first merfolk lived.
- **Everyday worship takes the form of dance and song (see Worship below)**.

## Worship: Dance and Song

- **Ancient times:** people worship through dance and song, which honours the gods rather than asking them for favours.
- A worship leader sets the rhythm and leads the singing.
- A small group of ordinary, untrained people dance, perhaps because the dance runs in their family or they feel called. Everyone else watches, claps or joins the song.
- Each region has its own dances for its favourite god, such as flowing, wave-like movements for the water god. The whole world shares dances for the Supreme Creator, the Father and the Mother.
- Bards could be seen as holy figures.
- **Modern day:** worship has drifted far from its ancient form. Dance and song are rare, which makes the gods feel even more distant.

### When People Dance

- **Set occasions:** seasonal dances, such as a dance after planting to hope for rain.
- **Celebrations:** dances to celebrate life.
- **In times of need:** a family in dire need may dance to ask for help.

### The Creation Festival

- A worldwide festival that reenacts the creation story. The worship leader sings of the Father of All passing each request to the Supreme Creator, and the dancers act out each gift as it arrives.
- No one knows the world's true age or the real anniversary of creation, so the date varies by culture. Everywhere holds it within roughly the same month.
- The festival is shared, but each region tells the story a little differently, putting its own favourite god at the centre.
- To do: a name for the festival and which month it falls in.

### Why Worship Faded

- As people gathered into bigger cities, local traditions were slowly lost.
- Then came an age of questioning, a kind of revolutionary age, when people began asking why they worshipped.
- Because the gods are distant and their power is limited, a dance might bring only a small rain, not enough for a season. People saw little result and stopped taking part.

### Do the Dances Work?

- Only sometimes. Even an answered dance brings a small effect, such as a light rain.
- **Ancient times:** the gods answered far more often.
- **Modern day:** as belief faded, the gods came to see themselves as less important and largely went back to their old lives in the wider cosmos. They are only called back now and then, so responses are rare and smaller.
- This forms a cycle: weak answers led to doubt, and doubt led the gods to withdraw further.

### The Gods in the Modern Day

- How each god feels about fading worship depends on the god.
- **Modingua, as goddess of love:** wants to be more involved. The world is still medieval and most people marry and start families young, but a few races may be marrying later or having fewer children, which saddens her. Still open.
- **God of magic:** may have the most influence today, because his bloodline has spread so widely. He went from touching one tiny cult to being the most present god.
- Possible hook: this reversal could stir up jealousy among the Nine.

## Magic

- The god of magic personally visited a small cult and gave them magic. This was a rare event, since the gods are distant.
- Magic passes down **by bloodline** from that first cult.
- The site of that visit is a **holy site**, possibly with a city that grew up around it.
- Story hooks: noble houses guarding their lineage, forbidden marriages, faked ancestry, resentment of the magic-born.
- Possible link: the first cult may have been an ice age people, which would put the oldest bloodlines in that era.

## The Ice Age People

- Mortals existed during the ice age and are the ancestors of later peoples.
- They had naturally tougher skin, and possibly stockier builds, to suit the cold.
- Glaciers limited where they could live. Lower sea levels may have opened land bridges that later flooded, a source of legends about lost homelands and drowned cities.
- As the ice retreated they scattered, and different peoples branched off from them. They could be the origin of dwarves or goliaths.
- **In the modern day:** they have faded into legend, but traces remain, such as northern families with tough skin, ruins in old glacier valleys, folk tales of frozen-age giants, and scholars arguing over whether they were real.

## Races at the Start of the World

Five races, chosen by Modingua and placed in phase five. Each race starts from one homeland, then migrates, splitting into different cultures as it spreads. The ice age drives much of this migration. All names are placeholders.

1. **Humans:** four or five cultures, such as Osisu and Bardon's city. Some may echo ancient humans.
2. **Kurupal:** small shapeshifting mountain folk, a bit like dwarves crossed with tiki figures, living under the mountains. An ancient race slowly dying out.
3. **Elves:** the ice age hit them hard, so by the modern day they are rare and secluded.
4. **Stoneskins:** mountain dwellers who survive the ice age well. Not frost giants. They migrate widely and split into cultures, from icy peaks to mountains in tropical regions.
5. **Owl-antlered folk (owl folk and antlered folk combined):** nocturnal, secluded and excellent hunters. Looks: a humanoid body with an owl's head and eyes, and feathers. No wings. Taloned, owl-like feet, so they don't wear shoes, and they walk and run like raptor dinosaurs. They also have deer-like antlers. A rare race, seen as very close to the gods, since their owl and antler features echo the gods' true forms. Like most of the world, they have no single favourite god. They really are somewhat closer to the gods; how much is still open. Society: small family clans that hunt by night and rarely mix with outsiders. Most never leave, but a rare few travel the world as guides, hunters or omen-readers, which is how other races know of them.
6.

- Kurupal vs stoneskins: the Kurupal build settlements in big underground caverns, digging tunnels outwards. The stoneskins build cities of stone and ice on the highest peaks. The Kurupal see the mountains as sacred and believe the stoneskins shouldn't touch them, which fuels an ancient rivalry between the two races. During the ice age the stoneskins thrive and spread across the peaks while the Kurupal shelter below, so to the Kurupal it looks like the stoneskins used the freeze to seize sacred ground.
