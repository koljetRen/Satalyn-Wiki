The **Dyken** are a people of [[Dyken]], a dry desert land, known for philosophy, architecture and trade, whose society is built on learning.

## Appearance

The Dyken have golden-brown skin, with hair ranging from dark black to auburn.

## Skills and livelihood

The Dyken are philosophers, architects and traders.

## Clothing

| Who | Clothing |
| --- | --- |
| Men (daily) | Draped chitons with sashes. The color of the chiton shows a man's role in society, and the color of the sash shows his level of knowledge. |
| Women (daily) | Long flowing chitons with silk scarves. |
| Guards | Bronze breastplate, shin guards and forearm guards, with capes whose colors show which region of the land they serve. |

## Society and gender roles

Men are usually traders, scholars, philosophers or guards. They are expected to dedicate their lives to learning and to take part in public events. Women often manage small family shops and are seen as the "guardians of culture and tradition", since they teach the culture to their children, but they aren't seen as public figures.

## Architecture and settlements

Dyken cities are built of marble in the style of ancient Athens. Temples are the largest buildings, topped with domes lined in gold. Homes are rectangular. Large amphitheatres serve as meeting places for the judges and for the priests' lectures. The amphitheatres, temples and other gathering places with plenty of seating stand at the center of each city, and the homes spiral outward from them.

## Government

Each city is a city-state ruled by a council of judges. When a meeting is held, no man of the city is denied entrance or a voice. The council takes the input of the townsmen and of the judges and makes the decisions.

Every five years each city-state elects one representative to run for the presidency. Behind closed doors, all the judges of all the city-states meet, listen to the representatives, and elect one of them **president of Dyken**.

## Religion

Every city has temples, which also serve as schools for children and adults alike. The priests and acolytes who look after the temples are worship leaders and teachers, and practice philosophy in their own time. The Dyken believe the highest calling is that of a scholar, and being granted the role of priest at the temple is the highest fulfillment a person can achieve.

[[Gehflaun]], Goddess of Intelligence, is central to Dyken worship. Her gift of knowledge has a flaw that the Dyken feel more strongly than anyone: [[Krarkur]], God of Deceit, twisted intelligence into castes and discrimination. This is felt across the world, but most of all among the Dyken, whose society relies on intelligence.

## Entertainment and customs

People in Dyken cities often take part in study activities, such as knowledge sharing, philosophical debates and study groups. They also play various board games.
