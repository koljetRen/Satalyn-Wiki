**The Ice Age** is the great freeze of roughly 1,000 years that began phase seven of creation, burying the north and south under ice sheets, ending the [[The Golden Age|golden age]] and turning its first cities into ruins.

## Background

By the rules of the Book of Creation, every world's seventh phase begins with a great freeze, so the ice age arrived right after life did. In phase seven the Supreme Creator steps away, and his influence on the world becomes minimal to none. The ice age is a designed part of creation, which is why it is far shorter than Earth's.

[[The Nine]] came from the lowest caste and didn't know this rule, so they planned their gifts for a mild world that was about to freeze. [[Lebarka]], the Father of All, almost certainly knew, since he had asked for a world and would have seen the rules in action. He didn't warn them — partly because he assumed they already knew (the freeze is common knowledge at his rank), and partly as a quiet test of whether they could really rise to a higher caste.

## What happened

- **The freeze.** There were no ice caps at the end of phase six. Once the freeze began, ice sheets spread over the north and south within 100 to 150 years: fast, but slow enough that people could flee rather than die instantly.
- **A fallen civilisation.** The gifts of phase six had let the first cities rise. The freeze hit them: fields froze over and the early cities were abandoned. The [[Ice Age People|ice age people]] were a fallen civilisation, not cavemen.
- **Land and sea.** Glaciers limited where people could live. Lower sea levels may have opened land bridges that later flooded — a source of legends about lost homelands and drowned cities.
- **The races.** The ice age drove much of the early migration of the five races:
  - the [[Elves]] were hit hard, and are rare and secluded by the modern day;
  - the [[Stoneskins]] survived it well, thriving and spreading across the peaks;
  - the [[Kurupal]] sheltered below the mountains, and to them it looked as if the stoneskins used the freeze to seize sacred ground — the root of an ancient rivalry.
- **The end.** After about 1,000 years the ice age ended, and the ice took another 150 to 200 years to retreat to its present-day extent.

## Aftermath

As the ice retreated, the ice age people scattered and different peoples branched off from them. Traces remain in the modern day: northern families with tough skin, ruins in old glacier valleys, folk tales of frozen-age giants, and scholars arguing over whether the ice age people were real.
